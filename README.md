# SlotSeeker

Where do I get a bigger bag than the smallest one I carry, at my level and with my professions?
SlotSeeker compares the bags you have equipped with every bag on WoW: Forever and lists the ones
you can get: from a vendor, a quest, your own craft, the auction house or a drop. Reachable ones
come first; a quest that is still too hard for your level sinks lower in the list instead of
disappearing.

- **The list** (`/seek`): bag, slots, how many you gain, which slot it goes in, the easiest source,
  how far out of reach it is, the quest or creature level, where to go, the vendor price, the
  auction house price and the price per slot gained. Filter it as text (`[Gain] >= 4 AND [Reach]
  = Now`), sort on any column (Ctrl-click for a second one). A click on a row sends you to the
  nearest vendor or quest giver through your route or waypoint addon; Shift-click puts the bag's
  link in chat.
- **The tooltip**: under any bag, `+4 slots over your smallest bag`, or that it is no bigger.
- **Slots compared**: the four bag slots (against your smallest plain bag; an empty slot counts as
  none), the reagent slot (reagent and profession bags), a hunter's quiver or ammo pouch and a
  warlock's soul bag.
- **Prices**: the vendor's price comes with the data; the auction house price from
  [Auctionator](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/auctionator) when you have it.
- **With [Questie](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/questie)**: quest and NPC names in your
  client's language, and quest givers and spawn spots the data lacks, from its QuestieDB.

## Commands

| | |
|---|---|
| `/seek`, `/slotseeker` | show or hide the list |
| `/seek options` (or `config`) | the options in their own window; also in Settings > AddOns |

## How it works

### Which bag a bag replaces

`Core\Bags.lua` reads the bag slots once after every `BAG_UPDATE_DELAYED`. The client's bag
family (`C_Container.GetContainerNumFreeSlots`'s second return) tells a plain bag (0) from a
quiver (1), an ammo pouch (2) or a soul bag (4), so a bag the data does not know still counts.
Per group the baseline is the smallest plain bag in slots 1-4, the bag in the reagent slot (5),
or the biggest quiver/ammo pouch or soul bag equipped. Quivers count for hunters only, soul bags
for warlocks only.

### Sources and reach

`Core\Upgrades.lua` gives every bag bigger than its baseline its easiest source: owned
(unequipped in your bags), vendor, quest, craft, auction house (anything that does not bind on
pickup), drop. A source has a tier:

| tier | when |
|---|---|
| Now | a vendor or the auction house; a quest up to two levels above you; a craft at your skill |
| Hard | a quest or creature three or four levels above you; a craft above your skill |
| Too hard | five or more above, or a quest you cannot take yet |

The bands are the quest log's colours. A lower tier wins, then less effort. The list sorts by
tier, then slot group, slots gained, effort and name. Vendors and quests of the other faction,
and bags of another class, are left out.

### Data

`Data\Forever\Bags.lua` is generated by a local parser that respects Wowhead's rate limits, from
Wowhead's Forever database: every container and quiver with its size, kind, vendors with their
prices, reward quests with their level and giver, crafts with their skill, named drops (three
droppers at most) or a world drop, and the NPCs' spawn spots. Test and GM bags (no source) are
left out. A comment over a bag says how it differs from Classic Era where Wowhead says so
("Bag slots 8 instead of 4"). `tests\data_spec.lua` checks that every reference in it resolves.

### QuestieDB

`Core\Questie.lua` reads QuestieDB, when installed, through its public API `LibQuestieDB`
(contract 4, checked with `RequireContract`), at run time only: QuestieDB is GPL-3.0 and none of
its data is copied into this MIT addon. It gives quest and NPC names in the client's language,
a quest's first NPC starter where the data has no giver, and an NPC's spawns where the data has
no pins. Its spawns are per AreaID; its ZoneDB support module's `areaIdToUiMapId` tables (Lua
text, read once) give the uiMapID LibGoThere takes. Every read goes through `pcall`: a failing
or incompatible QuestieDB leaves the addon's own data.

### Saved data (`SlotSeekerDB`, AceDB)

Per profile: `tooltip`, and the list window's `window` (place and size), `filter` (LibDataGrid's
filter tree) and `sort`. Account-wide: `dev` in a development copy (screenshots below).

### Messages

`<NAME>_UPDATED` (AceEvent `SendMessage`) after the list was worked out again, with the list
(`AddonRow[]`, see `annotations\Addon.lua`).

### Screenshots while testing in game

`Core\Dev.lua` is one `--@debug@` block, so only a copy straight from the repository has it.
Switched on in the options' Development box, it takes one screenshot the first time each kind of
moment from the in-game checks happens: `window`, `window: nothing bigger`, `tooltip: gain`,
`tooltip: no gain`, `route`, `options window`. A grey `[SlotSeeker dev] <kind>` chat line comes
first; at most one every two seconds; kept across sessions until "Forget the screenshots taken".

### Renaming

Every name the game and other addons see is made from the folder's name, `Addon.NAME`
(`Core\Addon.lua`): the saved variables (`<NAME>DB`), the window's frame (`<NAME>Window`), the
slash command (`/<name>` in lower case, plus `SLASH_ALIAS` `/seek`), the message
(`<NAME>_UPDATED`), the locale, the options page and LibGoThere's owner. `tests\names_spec.lua`
loads the addon as "Tester" and checks that these follow. The code carries no name: the addon
object is `Addon`, its types are `Addon...` in `annotations\Addon.lua`.

By hand: the folder and the repository, the `.toc`'s file name, `## Title` and
`## SavedVariables`, `.wowluarc.json`'s global, the rockspec, and the texts (locales, README).

## Development

Libraries come from the `.pkgmeta` externals: Ace3 (pinned commit, as in FishCatchVisualizer),
LibDataGrid-1.0 and LibGoThere-1.0 (the commits Huntsworn pins). `tests\check.lua` fetches them
into `Libs\` and runs StyLua, WoW Lua LS (100% type coverage) and busted (100% line coverage);
`tests\setup-lua.ps1` builds the tools on Windows. `tests\wow.lua` is the simulated client:
LibDataGrid's schema and view run for real, its widgets and LibGoThere's routing are stand-ins
that record what they get.

What only the real client can tell is tested in the game too: `InGame\Tests.lua` holds
[WoWUnit](https://github.com/Jaliborc/WoWUnit) tests that run at login when WoWUnit is
installed, in development copies only. `tests\ingame_spec.lua` runs the same tests on the test
client, so a test that fails in the game points at the client.
